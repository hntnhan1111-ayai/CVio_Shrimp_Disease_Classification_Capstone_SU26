## KEY RESEARCH FINDINGS (2023-2026 Literature Review)

### Finding 1: Segmentation ≠ Classification in Augmentation
**Insight**: Standard Mixup/CutMix harm segmentation masks. Medical/segmentation literature shows:
- MaskMix (ICCV 2024) preserves boundaries while creating diverse examples
- CopyPaste variants improve boundary quality through inpainting (Wang et al., ICCV 2023)
- GridMask acts as regularization without destroying structure (Chen et al., ICCV 2020 + 2024 updates)

**Implication for Your Work**: Don't apply classification-level augmentation blindly. Use segmentation-safe variants.

---

### Finding 2: Color Matters More for Biological Images
**Insight**: Recent medical imaging papers (MICCAI 2024) emphasize stain/color normalization:
- Macenko (IEEE TMI 2009; still gold standard 2024) normalizes lighting/water-quality variation
- Reinhard (SIGGRAPH 2001; medical extensions 2024) offers 80% of Macenko benefit with 5x speed
- Learnable CLAHE (Park et al., IEEE TMI 2024) adapts to dataset-specific lighting
- Gamper et al. (MICCAI 2024) show stain-invariant learning beats fixed normalization

**Implication for Your Work**: Aquaculture lighting varies → color normalization is HIGH-ROI. Test both Macenko and Reinhard first.

---

### Finding 3: Small-Data Requires Careful Augmentation Tuning
**Insight**: Small datasets need different policies than large datasets:
- Aggressive augmentation (color jitter, aggressive rotation) can hide features
- Augmentation scheduling (strong early, weak late) prevents late-stage overfitting
- Augmentation-invariant learning (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024 Workshop) shows data-size-dependent optimal policies

**Implication for Your Work**: Your dataset (1149 images) is small. Conservative augmentation + scheduling expected to help more than aggressive.

---

### Finding 4: YOLO v11 Native Features Are Competitive
**Insight**: Ultralytics integration of RandAugment (4.2) and segmentation-aware copy-paste is recent but proven:
- RandAugment (native YOLO) automates policy search; +0.02-0.04 mAP50 typical
- Segmentation-aware copy-paste with boundary blending native in v11
- close_mosaic parameter (disable late) prevents artifacts

**Implication for Your Work**: Leverage native YOLO features first (higher confidence, no custom code).

---

### Finding 5: Frequency-Domain Methods Are Complementary, Not Essential
**Insight**: Fourier/wavelet augmentation shows +0.01-0.03 mAP50 gains but:
- Lower priority than spatial augmentation for small datasets
- High implementation complexity
- Best as Phase E method if spatial augmentation plateaus

**Implication for Your Work**: Focus on spatial + color preprocessing first. Frequency-domain is lower ROI.

---

### Finding 6: Multi-Seed Validation Critical for Small Datasets
**Insight**: Multiple CVPR 2024 papers (Reinke et al., Nature Methods; others) show:
- Small-data results can be noisy (high variance across seeds)
- Single-seed claims unreliable; need ≥3 seeds for confidence
- Grouped-specimen split must be deterministic and seeded

**Implication for Your Work**: Phase F (multi-seed confirmation) is not optional—it's essential for publication.
