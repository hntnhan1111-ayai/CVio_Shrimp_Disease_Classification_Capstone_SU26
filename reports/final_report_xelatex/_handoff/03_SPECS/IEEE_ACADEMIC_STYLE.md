# IEEE-quality academic style adapted to an FPT thesis

The report is not an IEEE submission template. Use IEEE guidance for technical prose, mathematics, citations, and references while preserving the FPT institutional thesis layout.

## Technical English
- Prefer direct, precise, simple technical sentences.
- Define acronyms at first use.
- Keep claims close to the evidence that supports them.
- Avoid decorative or promotional language.
- Avoid unsupported “novel”, “state-of-the-art”, “universally superior”, “field-ready”, or clinical claims.

## Evidence wording
Preferred:
- “under the recorded protocol”
- “observed improvement”
- “supports”
- “is consistent with”
- “condition-dependent”
- “does not establish”
- “remains a limitation”

Distinguish observation, comparison, interpretation, hypothesis, and causal proof.

## Mathematics
Follow the official IEEE Math Typesetting Guide:
- variables italic;
- vectors bold;
- functions/operators upright;
- use modern AMS display environments;
- do not use `eqnarray` or `$$`;
- break long formulas at meaningful mathematical relations/operators;
- preserve consistent notation between prose and equations.

## References
- numeric bracket citations;
- one bibliographic item per reference number;
- use references that directly support the claim;
- avoid padding the bibliography;
- use semantic bibliography management rather than hard-coded citation numbers in prose;
- do not allow malformed artifacts such as `[ 1], 2], 3]`.

## Thesis-specific exception
IEEE article equation numbering is not imposed here.
Preserve chapter-based thesis numbering such as `(4.1)` because the FPT thesis structure is the page-layout authority.
